ANOVA: Comparing Averages Across Three or More Groups

Do email, social and search campaigns bring in orders of the same value, or does one channel really stand out? With two groups we could use a t-test. With three or more groups we need a different tool.

ANOVA (analysis of variance) tests whether the average of a numeric variable differs between three or more groups. It follows the same logic as Hypothesis Testing and extends the two-group comparison from T-Test vs. Z-Test.

Why Not Run Several t-Tests?

Two types of ANOVA

  • One-way ANOVA. It looks at one factor. Example: does the average order value differ between the three marketing channels (email, social, search)? There is one question, one grouping variable and three groups.
  • Two-way ANOVA. It looks at two factors at the same time. Example: does the average order value differ by marketing channel and by customer type (new vs. returning)? It tests three things in one analysis:
    • the effect of the channel
    • the effect of the customer type
    • the interaction between the two. Email might work well for returning customers but not for new ones, so the channel effect depends on the customer type.

This post walks through one-way ANOVA step by step. Two-way ANOVA builds on the same logic and is covered in a short section at the end.
