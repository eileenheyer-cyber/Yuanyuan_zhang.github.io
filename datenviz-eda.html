Exploratory data analysis

This analysis explores the NordHome retail dataset — customers, orders, order items, payments and returns — to understand how spending is distributed, how customer groups differ and where returns deserve a closer look. The aim is not to prove a hypothesis but to find patterns that lead to better questions.

Six of these 21 questions are walked through in detail below, in the order they were asked.

Deep dive 01How are order values distributed?

Spending is strongly right-skewed

Most orders sit in a narrow range, while a small number extend far beyond it: the median order is €658, the mean €787.

I kept the 717 flagged orders (2.5%) in — excluding them moves the average order value by only 6.3%.

→ So who is doing the spending? Next: repeat customers versus one-time buyers.

Deep dive 02Do repeat customers spend more?

Repeat customers show a substantially different spending pattern

85.2% of customers ordered more than once. They spend €2,845 on average across 3.6 orders, versus €800 for one-time buyers — about 3.6× more per customer.

→ Repeat buying separates customers clearly. Can a simple demographic such as age do the same?

Deep dive 03Does age separate high-value customers?

Age groups show limited differentiation

Average revenue per customer runs from €2,465 (50–59) to €2,617 (30–39) — a spread of roughly 6%. Age alone does not separate customers by spend.

→ If customer traits explain little, the focus moves to where orders come back: returns by channel and country.

Deep dive 04How do return rates differ by channel and country?

Order return rates cluster around 19% across channels and countries

Across 40 channel × country combinations, order return rates run from 16.0% to 22.8% (average 19.0%). The highest cell, Marketplace in the Netherlands, rests on roughly 750 orders and may be sampling noise; a proportion test would be the next step.

→ Return rates look similar across segments. Do they at least stay stable over time?

Deep dive 05How have return rates developed over time?

Return rates increased in H1 2024

The item return rate held at 8.2–8.7% from 2021 to 2023, then reached 9.7%; the revenue return rate moved from about 4% to 4.5%. A signal to investigate, not a conclusion.

→ Before asking which products might drive returns, one more basic question: which products carry the revenue?

Deep dive 06Which products generate the most revenue?

A small group of products dominates the top revenue range

Gifts account for 6 of the 10 best-selling products, including the top three. The ten sit in a tight band: the best earns about 1.6× the tenth (€144K vs €92K).

→ These deep dives are brought together in the key findings below.

Method note

Unit prices are capped at €250 and the average quantity is exactly 3.00, so order values should not be benchmarked against real retail. Observed differences are exploratory signals rather than causal conclusions. 2024 covers January–June only.

05 — Methods

How the analysis was performed

  • Data preparationLoaded the raw NordHome tables and checked their structure and row counts.
  • Data cleaningRemoved duplicates and flagged data-quality issues; excluded cancelled, returned and refunded orders where a chart requires it.
  • Exploratory analysisDescriptive statistics across six areas — revenue, customers, products, payments, returns and marketing — through 21 business questions.
  • Distribution analysisHistograms of order value and revenue per customer; median compared with mean.
  • Group comparisonsRepeat versus one-time buyers, age groups and top products.
  • Relationship analysisReturn rates by channel × country and over time.
  • Outlier analysisIQR method (Q3 + 1.5 × IQR) to flag orders and customers outside the typical range; flagged cases were kept in the data.

06 — Conclusion

Where the investigation ends — for now

  1. 01

    What did the EDA reveal?

    Spending is skewed, and repeat customers spend about 3.6× more per customer than one-time buyers. Age, channel and country hardly separate customers, and returns edged up in H1 2024.

  2. 02

    What did it not explain?

    Why repeat customers spend more, what defines a high-value customer, and what drives the rise in returns. EDA shows patterns, not causes — and the dataset is synthetic.

  3. 03

    Why is further analysis needed?

    Each open question calls for a targeted follow-up — segmentation, product and category drill-downs, and tests of whether differences are statistically meaningful — before any business recommendation.
