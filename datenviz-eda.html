03 — Exploration

Exploring the data

Charts are grouped by EDA technique, not by business area.

DistributionsHow are order values distributed, and do a few extreme orders inflate the average?

Spending is strongly right-skewed

Most orders sit in a narrow range, while a small number extend far beyond it: the median order is €658, the mean €787.

I kept the 717 flagged orders (2.5%) in — excluding them moves the average order value by only 6.3%.

Comparisons & segmentsDo customers who buy more than once look different from one-time buyers?

Repeat customers show a substantially different spending pattern

85.2% of customers ordered more than once. They spend €2,845 on average across 3.6 orders, versus €800 for one-time buyers — about 3.6× more per customer.

Comparisons & segmentsDoes age separate high-value from low-value customers?

Age groups show limited differentiation

Average revenue per customer runs from €2,465 (50–59) to €2,617 (30–39) — a spread of roughly 6%. Age alone does not separate customers by spend.

RelationshipsWhere do order return rates run highest, and is the variation meaningful?

Order return rates cluster around 19% across channels and countries

Across 40 channel × country combinations, order return rates run from 16.0% to 22.8% (average 19.0%). The highest cell, Marketplace in the Netherlands, rests on roughly 750 orders and may be sampling noise; a proportion test would be the next step.

A null result points the same way: unpaid payment value tracked processed value for every payment method (largest deviation +0.29 percentage points).

Change over timeHow have return rates developed year over year?

Return rates increased in H1 2024

The item return rate held at 8.2–8.7% from 2021 to 2023, then reached 9.7%; the revenue return rate moved from about 4% to 4.5%. A signal to investigate, not a conclusion.

Comparisons & segmentsWhich products lead on revenue, and which categories do they come from?

A small group of products dominates the top revenue range

Gifts account for 6 of the 10 best-selling products, including the top three. The ten sit in a tight band: the best earns about 1.6× the tenth (€144K vs €92K).

Outliers & anomaliesHow is revenue per customer distributed, and who falls outside the typical range?

A small number of customers fall outside the typical range

The typical range (Q1–Q3) of customer revenue runs from €1,216 to €3,446; the highest flagged customer reaches €11,428. The 1.9% refers to the 7,969 customers in the analysed base.

Statistical outlier ≠ business error

I used the IQR method (Q3 + 1.5 × IQR) to flag observations outside the typical distribution. An outlier differs statistically from the rest of the data — it is not automatically an error. Before it is classified as an error or a special customer group, it needs further investigation.

04 — Findings

What stood out

  1. 01

    Right-skewed spending

    Spending is concentrated toward the lower end, with a small number of high observations extending the distribution.

  2. 02

    Repeat ≠ one-time

    Repeat customers show substantially different spending patterns from one-time buyers (€2,845 vs €800 per customer).

  3. 03

    Limited differentiation

    Country, channel, age group, loyalty status and payment method show surprisingly similar patterns.

  4. 04

    Anomalies require context

    Statistical outliers are signals for investigation, not automatically errors.

Method note

The dataset is synthetic and intentionally contains more balanced distributions than real-world retail data. Observed differences should be read as exploratory signals rather than causal conclusions.

Unit prices are capped at €250 and the average quantity is exactly 3.00, so order values should not be benchmarked against real retail. 2024 covers January–June only.
