AlpenTech Controlling – Power BI Finance Reporting

A five-page controlling report in Power BI for the fictional AlpenTech GmbH, a German industrial B2B manufacturer — from plan-vs-actual variances through cash flow to a scenario analysis for 2026.

Role

Data modeling, DAX, report design

Context

Portfolio project for Controlling / FP&A

Technology

Power BI, DAX, Power Query, TMDL, Python

Status

Completed

Starting point

For the fictional AlpenTech GmbH, a controlling report was to be built that goes beyond a plain P&L: plan-vs-actual-vs-forecast comparison, cash flow and working capital, and a scenario analysis for the current fiscal year. Fiscal years 2023–2025 are shown in full, with 2026 as the open year — actuals through June, forecast through December.

Five report pages

  • Management Cockpit — core KPIs, EBITDA trend and revenue-to-net-income bridge
  • P&L Analysis — P&L by quarter, EBITDA bridge 2024→2025, margin trend
  • Budget & Variance — plan attainment, variance by cost center, biggest cost drivers
  • Forecast — mid-year projection, cash flow and working capital 2026
  • Scenario Analysis — base/downside/upside with data-anchored assumptions

Key findings (FY 2025)

Revenue hit plan at €20.16m, but EBITDA came in €1.12m below plan — the gap is cost-driven, with raw materials the biggest single driver at +€490k over plan. Gross margin eroded from 50.9% (2023) to 47.5% (2025), and €2.0m of EBITDA turned into only €391k of free cash flow.

2026 outlook

The mid-year forecast shows an operating result of −€107k against a plan of +€2.72m — the first loss in the dataset. About a third of the gap comes from the loss of the largest customer, two thirds from the structural cost trend in logistics, IT and energy.

Data model

Star schema with seven dimensions (date, company, business unit, cost center, account, customer, product) and seven fact tables for actuals, plan, forecast, working capital, capex and tax — plus a decoupled layout table that drives the P&L rows. Revenue is defined as positive, costs as negative, so SUM(amount) yields the operating result directly.

DAX in detail

  • Percentage variances are divided by ABS() of the base, so a cost overrun never wrongly shows up as positive
  • Forecast accuracy is measured only for the actual forecast period (KEEPFILTERS), not for months already known as actuals
  • Metrics that span accounts (margins, cost ratios) use REMOVEFILTERS ( dim_account )
  • 34 measures were renamed via a TMDL text transform and checked against all 122 field references in the report

Design decisions

Every non-obvious definition is documented with its rationale: COGS as direct production cost excluding depreciation (the only definition where gross profit minus operating expense lands exactly on EBITDA), a 75% cash-conversion target instead of 100% (the structural ceiling after tax sits at 77–81%), and fixed scenarios instead of free sliders, so they can be defended in front of management.

About the data

The dataset is synthetic and deliberately clean (fixed seed, reproducible) and is validated by script against keys, signs and scenarios. Every variance between plan, forecast and actual is a deliberate, explainable business scenario — the way a controller would investigate and report it.
